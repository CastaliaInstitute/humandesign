# Human Design semantic cues for flow interpretation

## Scope and evidence limits

This is a first-pass semantic inventory for asking whether an arrow can be justified by Human Design language. It is not a canonical source/sink map. The system treats gates as themed potentials, channels as connections with their own life-force quality, and centers as functional hubs. Those semantics can suggest a process, but they do not automatically establish a one-way particle route.

Jovian Archive describes gates as thematic activations, channels as connections formed by two gates, and circuitry/streams as patterns in which life force is organized. It also describes some specific directional relationships (for example, the Sacral energizing the Format channels, a Power Column from Sacral to G, and a Power Column from G to Throat). This supports using semantic evidence where it is explicit, not assigning every pair an arrow by number order.

### Evidence labels

- **Explicit stream clue:** a source describes a stream or column with an ordered direction or names one center as energizing another. This is the strongest evidence, though it may describe a multi-channel stream rather than prove a literal one-way conduit in each component channel.
- **Channel-theme clue:** the channel name or paired gate themes imply a sequence, cycle, or feedback relationship. Treat this as an interpretation unless a primary source states the direction.
- **Center-function clue:** motor, pressure, awareness, identity, or expression roles can help explain the endpoints, but by themselves do not decide direction.
- **No direction evidence recorded:** gate/channel meanings are useful context but the literature reviewed here does not justify a one-way arrow.

## Current implementation and implications

In `bodygraph.svg`, directed channels use `data-from` and `data-to` for the simulation source and sink. Channels marked `data-direction="undirected"` have no arrow and are traversable in either direction. The current recommendations reverse several earlier assignments (including 2–14, 16–48, and 20–34) and leave seven channels undirected; see [gate-direction-recommendations](gate-direction-recommendations.md) for the rationale and confidence labels. These remain visualization choices with mixed evidence, not canonical Human Design mechanics.

Before changing arrows, verify each candidate against the channel-specific source material, then mark each as **source-backed**, **interpretive**, **cyclic/feedback**, or **undirected**. Where Human Design describes a sequence such as “A informs B” and “B tests A,” a two-way or cyclic visualization may be more faithful than forcing a single arrow.

## Biological clues: useful analogies, not validation

Human Design literature gives biological associations to some Centers, but those are part of the system's interpretive map. For example, the Jovian Archive glossary associates the G Center with the liver, the Root with the adrenal system, the Sacral with reproductive function, the Splenic Center with immune/lymphatic function, and the Throat with thyroid function. These correspondences do not establish that the nine Centers are anatomical structures or that Human Design channels carry a measurable biological substance. The same glossary describes channels as carrying a quality or energy between Centers; that is Human Design terminology, not a demonstrated physiological transport pathway.

Established physiology offers several different, well-defined kinds of movement and signaling. They suggest ways to make the particle model more nuanced, but they do not combine into one universal source-to-sink map:

| Biological process | What actually moves / signal direction | Useful visualization clue | Limit on the analogy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neural circuits | Sensory afferents carry information toward the central nervous system; efferent pathways carry commands away; interneurons connect local circuit elements. | Use direction only when a particular circuit has a known input, processing step, and output. Circuits can include feedback. | There is no single body-wide direction shared by every nerve pathway, and the BodyGraph's abstract channels are not nerves. |
| Endocrine signaling | Glands secrete hormones into circulation; hormones reach many tissues, but receptor-bearing target cells respond. Hormone axes commonly use feedback. | A “source” can be a secreting tissue and a “sink” can be a receptor-bearing target for one named hormone. A feedback loop can return information to the source. | Hormones are molecules with specific production, transport, receptor, and clearance mechanisms; generic colored particles are not hormone representations. |
| Cardiovascular circulation | The heart pumps blood through pulmonary and systemic circuits, with a return path. | If modeling blood, show a closed loop, pressure-driven transport, and return flow rather than isolated one-way links. | The BodyGraph's Heart/Ego Center is not the anatomical heart, and its channels are not blood vessels. |
| Spleen | Blood enters through the splenic artery and drains through the splenic vein; the spleen filters aging/abnormal red cells and participates in immune responses. | A “sink” could represent selective capture/processing of a particle, while allowing carrier flow to continue through. | The spleen is not a terminal sink for all blood or a general-purpose absorber of energy. |
| Homeostatic control | Sensors, integrating/control elements, and effectors form feedback loops that compensate for changing conditions. | Model accumulation as a changing state (pressure, concentration, or load) that can trigger feedback, rather than deleting particles at an endpoint. | Pressure has a precise physical meaning only after the model defines what is conserved, where it accumulates, and how it changes motion. |

### Implications for this simulator

Biology supports a process-specific model, not a universal direction assigned to every Human Design channel. The strongest design choice is to keep two layers explicit:

1. **Human Design interpretation:** source-backed language about a channel's themes, streams, and endpoint roles, recorded in the semantic index above.
2. **Particle simulation:** a chosen visual metaphor with declared rules for generation, movement, attraction, accumulation, feedback, and exit.

For direction, use a documented channel-level Human Design clue when one exists; otherwise mark the channel as interpretive, cyclic/feedback, or direction-undetermined. For the physics metaphor, name what the particles stand for. If they are abstract “influence” or “attention,” do not imply they are hormones, blood, nerve impulses, or a biological energy field. If a later mode explicitly models a biological process, use that process's actual topology and transport rules instead of reusing all BodyGraph arrows.

This review found no biological basis for treating defined gates as anatomical emitters, undefined gates as anatomical ports, or open Centers as literal sinks. Those can remain intentional interaction rules in the visualization, provided the UI presents them as a speculative metaphor rather than physiology.
